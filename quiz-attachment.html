Your pattern leans toward

Anxious

Worry about relationships
Discomfort with closeness

You tend to want a lot of closeness and reassurance, and you may worry about whether people care as much as you do. Small signs of distance can feel big, and your mind can start to fill in the blanks.

What it can look like

  • Checking in often, or needing reassurance that things are okay
  • Overthinking a delayed text or a change in tone
  • Putting other people's needs first to keep the peace

Good to know

This is a learned pattern, not a flaw. Many people find that with practice, calming tools, and a better understanding of their triggers, they feel steadier in their relationships.

Your pattern leans toward

Fearful-Avoidant (sometimes called Disorganized)

Worry about relationships
Discomfort with closeness

You may want closeness and also feel nervous about it. Part of you wants to get near, and part of you worries about getting hurt, so you can swing between reaching out and pulling away.

What it can look like

  • Wanting connection but expecting to be let down
  • Feeling pulled in two directions in close relationships
  • Strong emotions when a relationship feels uncertain

Good to know

This pattern is common, especially after painful experiences. It is not a diagnosis, and it can change. Many people find that learning to calm their body and mind helps them feel safer in relationships. A good therapist can also help.
